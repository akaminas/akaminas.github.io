Non-indigenous species spread faster than formal surveys can follow. The observations of divers, fishers and the public, checked by taxonomists, help to fill the gaps: first records, range expansions and population densities.

From 2018 to 2023 I was a volunteer research fellow with the environmental organisation iSea, monitoring invasive species and elasmobranchs. I contributed to three citizen-science and public-engagement programmes: *Is it Alien to you? Share it!!!* (public engagement and alien-species monitoring under a research permit from the Greek Ministry of Environment), the marine alien-species population-density project for Greece and Cyprus, and the MECO collaborative network for elasmobranch sightings across the Mediterranean.

In 2020 a research internship at the Hellenic Centre for Marine Research, on the taxonomy, stomach contents and diet indices of commercial fish, contributed to a peer-reviewed paper on the feeding habits of *Dentex maroccanus*.

The work produced seven peer-reviewed articles (2020–2023), including a first-author paper reporting *Phyllorhiza punctata* in Cyprus, and two conference posters at HydroMediT 2021. It is data-gathering and monitoring rather than modelling, and it is the empirical grounding for my later work on marine communities.
